**Teacher:** Mufti Umar Aejaz

## Introduction

This lesson details the **Sunan of Ṭawāf (سنن الطواف)** [recommended acts of ṭawāf]: the **Istilām (استلام)** [greeting/touching] of **Ḥajar al-Aswad (الحجر الأسود)** [the Black Stone] at the start of every round, **Idtibāʿ (اضطباع)** [baring the right shoulder], **Ramal (رمل)** [brisk walking] in the first three rounds, and the differing rulings on greeting the four **corners (arkān)** of the Kaʿbah. It then covers the **two wājib rakʿahs** after ṭawāf (at Maqām Ibrāhīm), and the protocol of returning to Ḥajar al-Aswad for one more Istilām before beginning **Saʿī**.

---

## Part 1 — Istilām of Ḥajar al-Aswad in Every Round

Ṭawāf begins **from Ḥajar al-Aswad** and each round **ends at Ḥajar al-Aswad** — so every round begins there. At the start of each round he performs **Istilām**:

- **If possible without harming anyone:** touch it / kiss it — this was the **ʿādah (practice)** of the Prophet ﷺ.
- **If not possible:** simply **face it**, say **"Allāhu Akbar,"** and **raise the hands toward it** — like raising the hands to begin ṣalāh, but **only up to the shoulders** (not up to the ears).

Since **every round is a new round**, the Istilām is **masnūn in every round**, just like the first.

---

## Part 2 — Idtibāʿ (Baring the Right Shoulder)

In iḥrām there are two sheets — one covering the **lower** body, one covering the **upper** body. For ṭawāf, one performs **Idtibāʿ** with the upper sheet:

- Pass the upper sheet **under the right armpit/shoulder** (not over it), and leave it **over the left shoulder**.
- Result: the **right shoulder is exposed**, the **left shoulder is covered**.

**Etymology:** from **ḍabuʿ (ضبع)**, meaning the **ʿaḍud (عضد)** [upper arm] — because it exposes the upper arm/right shoulder in iḥrām. Hence the name *Idtibāʿ*.

---

## Part 3 — Ramal (Brisk Walking in the First Three Rounds)

**Ramal** is a manner of walking: **stretching out the shoulders** and walking **a bit faster** (with vigour).

- Done in the **first three rounds** of ṭawāf, **when one intends to perform Saʿī after the ṭawāf**.
- In a **crowd** where one cannot stretch the shoulders, take one's time — and perform it **when space is available**.

---

## Part 4 — Istilām of the Corners (Arkān)

The Kaʿbah has four corners. Their rulings differ:

### Rukn al-Yamānī (the corner reached just before Ḥajar al-Aswad)

Three opinions on greeting **Rukn al-Yamānī (الركن اليماني)**:

| Imam | Ruling |
|------|--------|
| **Imam Abū Ḥanīfah** | **Mustaḥabb** (recommended) — *not* masnūn |
| **Imam Muḥammad** | **Masnūn** (sunnah) |
| **Imam al-Shāfiʿī** | **Istilām + Takbīr + Taqbīl (kissing)** — all masnūn |

- In the **Ḥanafī** school, only the **Istilām** (not kissing) is at issue.
- **Evidence for it being sunnah (Imam Muḥammad):** it is narrated (from Ibn ʿUmar and others) that the Prophet ﷺ performed Istilām of **these two** — Ḥajar al-Aswad and Rukn al-Yamānī — and not the other two corners. (Ibn ʿAbbās رضي الله عنهما reported the Prophet ﷺ would even place his cheek upon it.)
- **Evidence for it being only mustaḥabb (the other Ḥanafī view):** there is a difference in **daraja (degree)** — **mustaḥabb is lower**, **sunnah is higher**. Had it been a full sunnah like Ḥajar al-Aswad, the **kissing (taqbīl)** would have been attached to it too. Since it is not, the Istilām of Rukn al-Yamānī is a **step below** that of Ḥajar al-Aswad — hence **mustaḥabb**.

### Rukn al-ʿIrāqī and Rukn al-Shāmī (the other two corners)

These come **after** Ḥajar al-Aswad. **No Istilām** is done for them — the view of the **majority of the Ṣaḥābah**, adopted by the **Aḥnāf**. (A minority of Ṣaḥābah held all four corners recommended.)

**Why the majority view is stronger — two arguments:**

1. **No basis in the Sunnah:** greeting these two did **not** come from the Prophet ﷺ; it was the practice of **some Ṣaḥābah**. An ʿibādah is established on the basis of **naṣṣ / the Prophet's ﷺ practice**, which is absent here.
2. **They are not true corners:** the word **rukn (ركن)** literally means "corner." But these two are **not on the actual foundation** of the Baytullāh — because the **Ḥaṭīm / Ḥijr (الحطيم / الحِجر)** on that side is **part of the Baytullāh** yet was **not included in the (later) construction**. So these two are really the **middle of the wall**, not genuine corners. Istilām, by contrast, is for Ḥajar al-Aswad and for a true corner (Rukn al-Yamānī).

> **Consequence:** because the Ḥaṭīm is part of the Baytullāh, it is **compulsory to make ṭawāf around the Ḥaṭīm as well** — one may **not** pass **between** the wall of the Baytullāh (on that side) and the Ḥaṭīm.

## Part 5 — The Two Rakʿahs of Ṭawāf

After completing all **seven rounds**, he prays **two rakʿahs**:

- **Better** performed near **Maqām Ibrāhīm (مقام إبراهيم)** if easily possible; otherwise **anywhere in Masjid al-Ḥarām**.
- These rakʿahs are **wājib**.

### Wājib vs. Sunnah (Ḥanafī vs. Shāfiʿī)

- **Imam al-Shāfiʿī:** since the Shāfiʿī school has **no "wājib" category** (a thing is either **farḍ** or **sunnah**), and these are not farḍ, they fall under **sunnah** (the Prophet ﷺ performed them consistently).
- **Ḥanafī:** three categories — **farḍ** (obligation from a **definitive/qaṭʿī** dalīl), **wājib** (obligation from a **probable/ẓannī** dalīl), and **sunnah**. These two rakʿahs are **wājib**.

### Evidence

**Qur'ān:**

> وَاتَّخِذُوا مِن مَّقَامِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ مُصَلًّى
>
> *"And take the Station of Ibrāhīm as a place of prayer."*
>
> **Ref:** al-Baqarah: 125

- This is a **command**, and a command minimally demands **wujūb**. But the dalīl is **not perfectly explicit** about *these two rakʿahs specifically*, so it cannot make them **farḍ** — hence **wājib**.

**About Maqām Ibrāhīm:** it is the **stone bearing the marks of Ibrāhīm's ﷺ feet**, upon which he used to stand (when dismounting/mounting his animal) while visiting Hājar and Ismāʿīl عليهما السلام, whom he had left in Makkah by Allah's command. **ʿUmar رضي الله عنه** expressed the wish that it be taken as a place of prayer — and the āyah was later revealed accordingly (one of the *muwāfaqāt* of ʿUmar).

**Ḥadīth:** when the Prophet ﷺ finished ṭawāf, he came to **Maqām Ibrāhīm**, prayed **two rakʿahs**, and **recited this āyah** as the reference for his act — showing the āyah refers to the two rakʿahs of ṭawāf **(hadith as cited in class — confirm in hadith index)**.

**Practice of ʿUmar رضي الله عنه:** he once **forgot** the two rakʿahs of ṭawāf and later performed them at a place called **Dhū Ṭuwā** — indicating they are **wājib** (otherwise there would be no need to make them up).

## Part 6 — Returning to Ḥajar al-Aswad Before Saʿī

After the two rakʿahs — and **before beginning Saʿī** — he returns to **Ḥajar al-Aswad** and performs **Istilām once more**. So the **start of Saʿī mirrors the start of ṭawāf**.

**Ruling:** wherever a **Saʿī follows the ṭawāf** (as here, with the intention of Saʿī), the protocol is: go to Ḥajar al-Aswad → Istilām → then Saʿī. If there is **no intention of Saʿī** after this (optional) ṭawāf, there is **no need** to return to Ḥajar al-Aswad.

- **Another (non-adopted) view:** reported that he does **not** return to Ḥajar al-Aswad — he goes to Saʿī directly. **This is not taken in the Ḥanafī school.**
- **Ḥanafī dalīl:** the Prophet ﷺ **returned and performed Istilām** of Ḥajar al-Aswad. Because **Saʿī is dependent on the ṭawāf**, it should be **begun the same way ṭawāf is begun** — with Istilām.

### The gap (faṣl) between ṭawāf and Saʿī

It is **makrūh** to make a **faṣl (فصل)** [gap] between ṭawāf and Saʿī. **Question:** doesn't going back for the Istilām create a gap? **Answer:** this Istilām is **counted as part of the ṭawāf**, not a gap — so it does not break the connection. (This applies specifically when he is doing **Saʿī after the ṭawāf**.)

---

## Part 7 — Beginning Saʿī (Preview)

After the Istilām, he proceeds to **al-Ṣafā** and begins **Saʿī from Ṣafā to Marwah** — the order in which the Qur'ān mentions them:

> إِنَّ الصَّفَا وَالْمَرْوَةَ مِن شَعَائِرِ اللَّهِ
>
> *"Indeed, al-Ṣafā and al-Marwah are among the symbols of Allah."*
>
> **Ref:** al-Baqarah: 158

**Which gate to exit from?** It is **not specified**. A narration reports the Prophet ﷺ exited via **Bāb al-Ṣafā**, but per the Ḥanafī reading this was **coincidence** (it was simply closest to Ṣafā) — **not masnūn**. One may exit from **any gate**.
