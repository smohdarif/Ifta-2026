## Introduction

This lesson **completes** the discussion begun last class on **combining Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr (Jamʿ Taqdīm / جمع تقديم)** at ʿArafāt: the **evidence** of the **Ṣāḥibayn** (that combining is allowed even alone) against the **preferred view of Imam Abū Ḥanīfah** (that it is restricted to the **main congregation**); the detailed **cases** clarifying what "jamāʿah" and "imām" mean here; the condition of **being in the iḥrām of Hajj**; and the **khalīfah (substitute-imam)** scenarios. It then moves to the **Wuqūf at ʿArafāt itself** — the **ādāb and wording of duʿā'** — and the **continuation of the Talbiyah** through the Wuqūf.

---

## Part 1 — The Dalīls: Ṣāḥibayn vs. Imam Abū Ḥanīfah

### The Ṣāḥibayn's argument (combining is allowed even alone)

The **purpose** of combining is so the pilgrim can remain **uninterruptedly focused on the Wuqūf** without a **break (faṣl)**:

- If ʿAṣr were prayed **at its own time**, the pilgrim would have to **interrupt his Wuqūf/duʿā'** later to pray it — creating a **break**.
- So the Sharia permits praying **both together** (ʿAṣr brought early into Ẓuhr time), leaving **no break** in the Wuqūf.
- **This reason applies equally** whether one prays **with the jamāʿah** or **alone**. Hence the Ṣāḥibayn hold that combining is permitted **even for one praying alone**.

### Imam Abū Ḥanīfah's argument (restricted to the congregation) — *preferred*

- The **standard ruling (default qiyās)** is that a prayer **cannot be performed before its time**:

> **إِنَّ الصَّلَاةَ كَانَتْ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ كِتَابًا مَوْقُوتًا**
>
> *"Indeed, the prayer is an obligation upon the believers at fixed times."*
>
> **Ref:** al-Nisā', 4:103.

- We only **departed** from this default because of the **ḥadīth** (the Prophet's ﷺ practice at ʿArafāt). **Anything permitted by ḥadīth against the standard ruling must be restricted to the exact context of that ḥadīth.**
- **The context:** the Prophet ﷺ performed **both prayers with the (main) congregation** and brought ʿAṣr early **in that scenario**. Therefore combining is permitted **only when both prayers are performed with the jamāʿah**. If prayed **alone**, the **default ruling** applies — **no combining** — because the **context of the ḥadīth is no longer present**.

### Counter to the Ṣāḥibayn's "break" argument

The **ṣalāh itself is not a disturbance** of the Wuqūf — a prayer **cannot be counted as a "break" or interruption**. So the "avoid the break" reasoning does **not** hold: praying ʿAṣr at its own time is **not** a genuine interruption of the Wuqūf.

## Part 2 — Cases Clarifying "Both Prayers with the Jamāʿah"

### Case 1 — The latecomer who still "catches" both congregations

A man **joins the imam late**: the imam has completed **three rakʿahs of Ẓuhr** and is in the **fourth**. He:

1. prays **one rakʿah with the imam** (in Ẓuhr's 4th rakʿah), then
2. gets busy with the **qaḍā' of his three missed rakʿahs**.

Meanwhile the imam finishes Ẓuhr and begins ʿAṣr, completing its **1st, 2nd, and 3rd** rakʿahs, and is now in the **4th rakʿah of ʿAṣr**. This man, having just finished his missed Ẓuhr rakʿahs, then **joins the imam in the 4th rakʿah of ʿAṣr**.

**Ruling:** he has effectively **caught the jamāʿah of both prayers** — so **combining is permitted** for him (he may pray ʿAṣr early), because the condition (**both prayers with the jamāʿah**) is met.

### Case 2 — What "jamāʿah" means here (the debatable condition)

"**Jamāʿah**" here means the **main congregation led by the imam** — i.e. the **Amīr al-Ḥajj** (the ruling authority who leads, e.g. the Saudi prince) **or his appointee (nā'ib)**. A **private / personal jamāʿah does not count**. (This is a **debatable condition** between **Imam Abū Ḥanīfah** and the **Ṣāḥibayn**.)

- If a person **missed the main jamāʿah** and some people **gather to form their own (private) jamāʿah**, they **may pray** — **no problem** — **but they may not combine**: they pray **Ẓuhr at Ẓuhr's time** and **ʿAṣr at ʿAṣr's time**.
- If a person **quickly prays Ẓuhr in a private jamāʿah** and then **joins the imam for ʿAṣr**, he is **not** allowed to bring ʿAṣr early with the imam — he must pray **ʿAṣr at its own time**.
- **In short:** the concession of combining is **only** for those who prayed **both Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr with the main jamāʿah** (the Amīr's / his nā'ib's). Otherwise, each prayer is at its own time.
- **Basis (Abū Ḥanīfah):** the concession is **context-bound** — the ḥadīth's context was **both prayers performed by the Prophet ﷺ with the main jamāʿah**.

## Part 3 — The Leader (Imam) as the Main Condition

### Case 3 — The imam appoints someone to lead while he makes wuḍū'

The imam **delivered the khuṭbah**, then needed to make **wuḍū'**, so he **ordered another person to lead** Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr while he returned.

- That appointee **may lead validly** — **even though he did not deliver the khuṭbah** — because the **khuṭbah is not a condition / part of the ṣalāh** here.
- **Contrast with Jumuʿah:** there the khuṭbah **is a part** of the prayer, so one who did not deliver it cannot lead the Jumuʿah.

### Case 4 — A common person leads without the imam's order

If the imam **did not appoint anyone**, and a **common person takes it upon himself** to lead:

- That jamāʿah is **not valid** (for the combining concession), because here **being the imam — or the nā'ib of the imam — is a must**.
- **The Ṣāḥibayn disagree:** they do **not** require this — an **ordinary person may form the jamāʿah in the imam's absence** without his order.
- **Exception (agreed):** if the leading person is himself **among the authorities** — e.g. a **Qāḍī (judge)** or an **army commander** — he **may lead**, because he is a **capable/authorised imam (in the ḥukm of a nā'ib)**. So the condition is **being the imam or an authorised imam**.

### Case 5 — The imam breaks wuḍū' mid-prayer (Istikhlāf)

The imam was leading **Ẓuhr** and **broke his wuḍū' (ḥadath)** mid-prayer, so he appointed a **khalīfah (substitute)** who **completed the Ẓuhr from where he left off** and then **led the ʿAṣr** as well.

- Once the imam **appointed a khalīfah, he turned into a common person** — now treated like an ordinary follower; the **khalīfah became the imam**.
- If the original imam **returns and joins the khalīfah during ʿAṣr** → he **may** pray ʿAṣr early (combine) with everyone.
- If he returns **after ʿAṣr is finished** → he is **not** allowed to combine; he **missed the jamāʿah of ʿAṣr** and must **wait for ʿAṣr's time**.

---

## Part 4 — The Iḥrām Condition (Must Be in the Iḥrām of Hajj)

Another condition **extracted from the ḥadīth**: the person combining must be a **Muḥrim — in the iḥrām of Hajj**.

- The Prophet ﷺ was **in iḥrām** on that occasion; since we follow the ḥadīth **exactly as it came**, **being in iḥrām is required**.
- A person present at Zawāl **without iḥrām** is **not** allowed to bring ʿAṣr early with Ẓuhr.
- **The iḥrām must be the iḥrām of Hajj — not of ʿUmrah.**

### Case 6 — In iḥrām of ʿUmrah at Zawāl

A person prays with the imam's main jamāʿah while **in the iḥrām of ʿUmrah**. At Ẓuhr's time he was a Muḥrim **for ʿUmrah**; only **later**, after the prayer, does he **assume the iḥrām of Hajj**.

**Ruling:** he is **not** allowed to combine (bring ʿAṣr early) — he must **wait for ʿAṣr's own time**.

---

## Part 5 — The Imam, Not the Jamāʿah, Is the Core Condition

The **main condition** is the **presence of the imam (the leader or his nā'ib)** — the **jamāʿah is secondary**.

### Case 7 — The people leave the imam alone

If the people **abandon the imam** (he is left alone), he **may still combine** both prayers — because **being the imam is the condition**, and the jamāʿah being present was only a **coincidence**, not the core requirement.

### Q&A — If being imam is the condition, why can't the first imam (who appointed a khalīfah) combine?

**Q:** In Case 5, if **being imam** is the condition, the **original imam** should be allowed to bring ʿAṣr early even after ʿAṣr is done — since he "is the imam." Why is he not allowed?

**A:** Because **he is no longer the imam.** The moment he **appointed the khalīfah**, that person **became the imam** and he **became an ordinary person** — so he **cannot** combine.

### Case 8 — If the imam passes away

If the imam **passes away** mid-way, his **khalīfah takes his place** and **leads Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr combined** in Ẓuhr's time.

---

## Part 6 — After the Combined Prayer: Proceeding to the Wuqūf

After the combined **Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr (Jamʿ Taqdīm)**, the imam and the people proceed to the **field of the Wuqūf at ʿArafāt** to make duʿā' — as the **Prophet ﷺ did**.

---

## Part 7 — The Duʿā' at ʿArafāt: Its Ādāb

- **Face the Qiblah.**
- **Raise the hands**, stretched out with a **small space between them**, **parallel to the shoulders / upper chest** (the **level of the ṣadr**, where there is a slight hollow) — as the Prophet ﷺ did at ʿArafāt.
- Stand with **humbleness** — **like a poor person (miskīn) begging for food**. This is the **adab of duʿā'**, adopted here.
- **Duration:** a **long stay**, continuing until the **Ghurūb al-Shams (sunset)** — making **duʿā'**, **dhikr**, and **durūd** upon the Prophet ﷺ. **This time and place is when duʿā's are accepted** — so one may ask **whatever he wishes**, at length.

### The reported wording

**(1) The best duʿā' of this day:**

> **«خَيْرُ الدُّعَاءِ دُعَاءُ يَوْمِ عَرَفَةَ، وَخَيْرُ مَا قُلْتُ أَنَا وَالنَّبِيُّونَ مِنْ قَبْلِي: لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ، لَهُ الْمُلْكُ وَلَهُ الْحَمْدُ وَهُوَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ»**
>
> *"The best of duʿā' is the duʿā' of the Day of ʿArafah, and the best of what I and the Prophets before me have said is: 'There is no deity but Allah alone, with no partner; His is the dominion and His is all praise, and He is over all things capable.'"* (This is the **fourth kalimah**.)
>
> **Ref:** Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī, 3585 (as cited in class — confirm numbering).

**(2) An evening duʿā' of ʿArafah (with a slight variation), including seeking refuge:**

> **«… وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ شَرِّ مَا وَلَجَ فِي اللَّيْلِ … وَمِنْ شَرِّ مَا تَجِيءُ بِهِ الرِّيحُ»**
>
> *"…and I seek refuge in You from the evil of what enters in the night… and from the evil of what the wind brings."*

(Much of what is harmful **appears in the night**, and **winds/storms** can bring harm — so refuge is sought from both.)

### No fixed duʿā'

- These duʿā's are **recommended, not fixed** — one **makes these and freely adds his own**.
- The duʿā' is an **informal conversation between the servant and Allah** — there is **no formality**, so the wordings are **not fixed upon** the person.
- **Why not fix them:** memorising set Arabic wordings would force a person to **focus on the words** rather than on the **great day** itself. For one who **knows Arabic** and can memorise the reported duʿā's, that is **"a cherry on the cake"** — but it is **not required**.

## Part 8 — Continuing the Talbiyah Through the Wuqūf

- The pilgrim **keeps saying the Talbiyah from time to time** during the Wuqūf — he does **not** cut it off. Talbiyah is **among the best acts** of this occasion, said **alongside** the duʿā's.
- **Imam Mālik:** he **cuts off the Talbiyah** when he begins (the khuṭbah / the Wuqūf duʿā').
- **Preferred view here (the majority, incl. the Ḥanafīs — contrary to Imam Mālik):** the **Talbiyah continues**.
- **Until when?** Until the **Ramī of Jamrat al-ʿAqabah on the 10th** — at which point the Talbiyah is **cut off**.

**Dalīl — Ibn Masʿūd رضي الله عنه:** on the **evening of ʿArafah**, someone objected that "this is not the place for the Talbiyah." He responded:

> *"Have the people become ignorant, or have they forgotten? For I found the Messenger of Allah ﷺ saying the Talbiyah on this occasion."*
>
> **(narration as cited in class — confirm in hadith index)**

He did **not leave the Talbiyah until the Ramī of Jamrat al-ʿAqabah** — but he **mixed it** with other adhkār (**Tahlīl, Takbīr**).

### Why the Talbiyah continues (the parallel with the Takbīr of ṣalāh)

- The **Talbiyah is the dhikr with which this ʿibādah (Hajj) began** — like **"Allāhu Akbar"** which **opens the ṣalāh** and is then repeated **throughout it** (going down, coming up). Likewise the **Talbiyah is said continuously** until the **Ramī** is done.

### The one performing ʿUmrah alone

- He **begins ʿUmrah with the Talbiyah**, but **cuts it off when he begins the Ṭawāf** — at the **Istilām of al-Ḥajar al-Aswad**, before starting the rounds — and does **not** say it thereafter.
- **The parallel:** in **Hajj**, the Talbiyah is cut off **before the Ramī**; in **ʿUmrah**, it is cut off **before the Ṭawāf begins**.

---

## Part 9 — Facing the Qiblah During Duʿā', and the Imam's Position

- **Facing the Qiblah** during the duʿā' is **better if easily done** — the Prophet's ﷺ best duʿā' at ʿArafāt was made **facing the Qiblah**; this is the **normal adab of duʿā'**.
- **But it is not obligatory** — during the duʿā' one **may move about**, and the duʿā' is **not broken by turning to another side** (it is **not ṣalāh**, which would be invalidated by turning). If a person becomes **tired** or has some difficulty, facing the Qiblah is **not a must** (nor even specifically recommended) here.
- **Contrast:** facing a direction **is a must** in acts of Hajj **like Ṭawāf**, where the direction/manner is integral.
- **The imam** is best making duʿā' **while riding** (upon his mount) — the **Prophet ﷺ did the same**.
- **Being closer to the imam is better** — since the imam **teaches the people and makes duʿā' for them** — **if it can be done easily** (without pushing or harming others).
