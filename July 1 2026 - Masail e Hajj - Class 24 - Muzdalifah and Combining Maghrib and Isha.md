## Introduction

Continuing the method of Hajj from ʿArafāt, this lesson covers the **calm departure** after sunset, reaching **Muzdalifah (مزدلفة)**, and combining **Maghrib and ʿIshā'** there as **Jamʿ Taʾkhīr (جمع تأخير)** — both prayed in ʿIshā' time with **one adhān and one iqāmah** (the view of the three imams). It then examines the differing views (Zufar, Shāfiʿī), the rule that these two prayers **must be performed at Muzdalifah** (Usāmah's ḥadīth "*al-ṣalātu amāmak*"), the **excuse (ʿudhr)** cases, spending the **night** (Sunnah), the **early Fajr (Taghlīs)** of the 10th, the **Wuqūf** at al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām, and the departure to Minā — opposing the practice of Jāhiliyyah.

---

## Part 1 — Departure from ʿArafāt (with Sakīnah)

After sunset, pilgrims depart from ʿArafāt **calm and composed** — with **Sakīnah (سكينة)** and **Iṭmiʾnān (اطمئنان)** [tranquillity] — **without running or rushing**, until they reach Muzdalifah. The Prophet ﷺ did likewise, **restraining his mount** from going fast, and taught that **birr (righteousness)** is not in making one's horse or camel gallop, but in moving with calm and dignity.

> *"O people, be calm; righteousness is not in haste (making the mounts race)."*
>
> **(meaning of the ḥadīth as cited in class — confirm wording/number in hadith index; cf. the ḥadīth of calm departure, Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī)**

## Part 2 — Reaching Muzdalifah

- The purpose is to reach Muzdalifah and pray **Maghrib and ʿIshā' together** there — again moving with **sukūn and iṭmiʾnān**, not running.
- **Following the imam:** it is preferred to move **after the imam**. But if the imam **delays** until the night has clearly fallen and it is dark, the people **do not wait any longer** — there is no reason to wait — and proceed to Muzdalifah.
- **Where to stay:** anywhere in Muzdalifah — to the **right or left** of the road — but **not on the road itself**.
- **Where NOT to stay: Wādī Muḥassir (وادي مُحَسِّر)** — the Prophet ﷺ avoided it. (So: not on the road, and not in Wādī Muḥassir.)

---

## Part 3 — Combining Maghrib & ʿIshā' (Jamʿ Taʾkhīr): Adhān & Iqāmah

At Muzdalifah, the imam leads **Maghrib late** (in ʿIshā' time), then leads **ʿIshā' with no new adhān and no new iqāmah**.

### The difference from ʿArafāt

| Place | Combination | Adhān | Iqāmah |
|-------|-------------|-------|--------|
| **ʿArafāt** (Jamʿ Taqdīm) | Ẓuhr + ʿAṣr in Ẓuhr time | **One** | **Two** |
| **Muzdalifah** (Jamʿ Taʾkhīr) | Maghrib + ʿIshā' in ʿIshā' time | **One** | **One** |

### The scholars' views on Muzdalifah

| Imam | Ruling |
|------|--------|
| **Abū Ḥanīfah, Abū Yūsuf, Muḥammad** *(preferred)* | **One adhān + one iqāmah** for both |
| **Imam Zufar** | **One adhān + two iqāmahs** (comparing it to Jamʿ Taqdīm at ʿArafāt) |
| **Imam al-Shāfiʿī** | Held a different configuration (a second adhān) — against the three imams |

### Evidence and reconciliation

- **For one adhān + one iqāmah:** the narrations (from Jābir / Ibn ʿUmar and others) that the Prophet ﷺ prayed both **with a single adhān and single iqāmah**.
- **Imam Zufar's ḥadīth** mentions the Prophet praying "**with two**." There is thus an apparent **conflict** between the two ḥadīths, requiring **taʾwīl (reconciliation)**.
- **Resolution:** the "**two**" in Zufar's ḥadīth is understood as the **adhān and the iqāmah** (i.e. "*iqāmatayn*" used loosely for the two calls) — since **no separate adhān is mentioned**. This is **Taghlīb (تغليب)** — using one word for two things — a normal feature of Arabic:
  - **al-ʿUmarān (العُمَران)** = Abū Bakr **and** ʿUmar (the word "ʿUmar" covering both).
  - **al-Qamarān (القمران)** = the **sun and the moon** (the word "qamar/moon" covering both).
  - The ḥadīth **«بَيْنَ كُلِّ أَذَانَيْنِ صَلَاةٌ ... لِمَنْ شَاءَ»** — *"Between every two adhāns there is a prayer for whoever wishes"* (except Maghrib) — where "**two adhāns**" means the **adhān and the iqāmah**, not two literal adhāns. **Ref:** Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, 624; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, 838.

### Rebuttal of Zufar's analogy to ʿArafāt (Jamʿ Taqdīm)

- At **ʿArafāt**, the **second prayer (ʿAṣr)** is performed **before its own time** — so a **fresh iqāmah** is needed to announce that a prayer is being offered ahead of its time.
- At **Muzdalifah**, the **second prayer (ʿIshā')** is performed **within its own time** — so **no announcement** is needed; **the time itself is the announcement**. Hence **no second iqāmah**. The two cases are therefore not analogous.

### No gap between the two prayers

Nothing — **no nawāfil** or other activity — is done **between** Maghrib and ʿIshā'; they are prayed **one after another**, as the Prophet ﷺ did. If a person **breaks the continuity** (prays nafl or busies himself), he must make a **new iqāmah** for ʿIshā', because it has now become detached from the first announcement.

## Part 4 — Who May Combine? (Muzdalifah vs. ʿArafāt) — clarifying the previous class

A question from the previous class was clarified:

- **At ʿArafāt (Jamʿ Taqdīm):** combining is an **exception** permitted **only in the congregation of the Amīr al-Ḥajj** (or his appointee), because the **ʿAṣr is prayed before its time** — established strictly by the Prophet's ﷺ practice as leader. A person praying **alone or in his own small jamāʿah** (e.g. in a tent) may **not** combine at ʿArafāt; he prays **Ẓuhr at its time** and **waits for ʿAṣr's time**.
- **At Muzdalifah (Jamʿ Taʾkhīr):** both prayers are performed **after their times have entered** (Maghrib merely **delayed** into ʿIshā' time). So there is **no restriction to the Amīr's congregation** — **anyone** may combine, **alone or in a small jamāʿah**.
- **Best** at Muzdalifah is to pray with the imam/Amīr al-Ḥajj's congregation; but praying in one's own tent (alone or in a group) is **fine**, since the times have already come.

## Part 5 — Praying Maghrib/ʿIshā' Before Reaching Muzdalifah

**Question:** If a person prays **Maghrib after sunset** (or **ʿIshā' after its time**) while **still on the way**, before entering Muzdalifah — is it valid?

**This is a debatable case:**

| View | Ruling | Reasoning |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **Abū Ḥanīfah & Muḥammad** *(preferred)* | **Not valid — must repeat** at Muzdalifah (if he arrives before **Ṭulūʿ al-Fajr**) | These prayers **must** be performed at Muzdalifah; there is a **restriction of time and place** |
| **Imam Abū Yūsuf** | **Valid**, but he is a **wrongdoer (isāʾah)** for missing the Sunnah | The prayer was offered **within its time**; missing a Sunnah does not invalidate the ṣalāh |

**Dalīl (preferred view) — Usāmah ibn Zayd رضي الله عنه:** on the way to Muzdalifah the Prophet ﷺ made a short stop, relieved himself and made wuḍū'; Usāmah reminded him that the (Maghrib) time had come. The Prophet ﷺ replied:

> **«الصَّلَاةُ أَمَامَكَ»**
>
> *"The prayer is ahead of you."*
>
> **Ref:** Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, 1669; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, 1280.

He did not pray until he reached Muzdalifah — proving Maghrib/ʿIshā' **cannot be prayed ahead of Muzdalifah**:
- **Time restriction:** Maghrib is **not** prayed early (it is delayed to ʿIshā' time).
- **Place restriction:** Maghrib/ʿIshā' are **not** prayed **before** reaching Muzdalifah.

This is **obligatory to follow** (shown by the Prophet's ﷺ wording); one who violates it is **ordered to repeat** as long as ʿIshā' time remains before Fajr.

### If he did not repeat until Fajr entered

Then the two prayers **become valid**. This reconciles two sets of evidence:
- **General texts** (Qur'ān/aḥādīth) fix the **times** of Maghrib and ʿIshā' **without a place restriction** → they demand **validity**.
- **Usāmah's ḥadīth** ("*al-ṣalātu amāmak*") → demands **invalidity** (restricted to Muzdalifah).

**Tawfīq (reconciliation):** *before* Fajr, we act on Usāmah's ḥadīth (time + place restriction → repeat if violated); *after* Fajr enters, we act on the general texts (which fix the times without place) → so it **becomes valid**.

## Part 6 — The Excuse (ʿUdhr) Cases

- **Departure delayed to the very last part of the night:** if waiting to reach Muzdalifah would cause him to **miss ʿIshā' time entirely** (Fajr would enter), then out of **ʿudhr** he **may** pray Maghrib and ʿIshā' **outside Muzdalifah**.
- **Lost / cannot find the way:** he still may **not** pray outside Muzdalifah — he keeps **searching and waiting** until only the **last ~10–15 minutes** of ʿIshā' time remain; **only then** does he pray (wherever he is), so as not to miss the time.

---

## Part 7 — The Night at Muzdalifah (Sunnah)

- **Spending the night** at Muzdalifah — the night between the **9th and 10th of Dhū al-Ḥijjah** — is **masnūn**.
- If missed (with or without reason), he is a **wrongdoer** for missing the Sunnah, but **no penalty (dam)** is due — he missed no **wājib**.
- The **wājib** is the **Wuqūf at Muzdalifah after Ṭulūʿ al-Fajr** (as studied earlier), not the overnight stay itself.

---

## Part 8 — The Early Fajr (Taghlīs) of the 10th

When Fajr enters, the imam prays **Fajr early — in the darkness**. This is **Taghlīs (تغليس)** [praying in the dark].

- **Normally**, the **mustaḥabb** Fajr time in the Ḥanafī school is **Isfār (إسفار)** — praying when it has become **light** (the later part of the time).
- **On this day only**, the Prophet ﷺ prayed it **early/in the dark** (reported by **ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd رضي الله عنه**).
- **Why early?** To **extend the time for Wuqūf at Muzdalifah**. The Sunnah of Isfār can be attained on **any** day of the year, but this Muzdalifah Wuqūf comes **only on this day** — so Fajr is prayed early to gain **more time** for it (and because departure to Minā must be **before sunrise**).

---

## Part 9 — Wuqūf at al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām, then Departure to Minā

- After Fajr, the imam and people make **Wuqūf** — best **near the mountain Quzaḥ (قُزَح)**, i.e. **al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām (المشعر الحرام)**.

> فَاذْكُرُوا اللَّهَ عِندَ الْمَشْعَرِ الْحَرَامِ
>
> *"...remember Allah at al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām."*
>
> **Ref:** al-Baqarah: 198. (Ibn ʿAbbās رضي الله عنهما interpreted *al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām* as that mountain; some say it refers to the whole of Muzdalifah.)

- They continue **dhikr and duʿā'** until it is **very bright**, then depart for **Minā before sunrise**.

### Opposing the Jāhiliyyah

| Place | Jāhiliyyah practice | The Prophet's ﷺ opposition |
|-------|---------------------|-----------------------------|
| **ʿArafāt** | Left **before** sunset | Depart **after** sunset |
| **Muzdalifah** | Left **at/after** sunrise | Depart **before** sunrise |

One who departs from Muzdalifah **after Ṭulūʿ al-Shams (sunrise)** is a **wrongdoer**.
