# Masail e Hajj — Class 17 Summary

**Date:** June 15, 2026
**Topic:** Ḥalq / Qaṣr (Shaving or Trimming the Head) — Its Obligation (vs. Imam al-Shāfiʿī), the Qur'anic Evidences (Sūrat al-Ḥajj & Sūrat al-Fatḥ), the Superiority of Ḥalq, the Bald Person, the Muḥṣar, Women's Taqṣīr, and the Amount, Time & Place of Ḥalq
**Teacher:** Mufti Umar Aejaz

---

## Introduction

This lesson opens the chapter of **Ḥalq / Qaṣr (حلق / قصر)** [shaving or shortening the hair], one of the acts of **Yawm al-Naḥr (the 10th)**. It covers: whether Ḥalq is **wājib** (Ḥanafī) or merely a means of exiting iḥrām (Imam al-Shāfiʿī); the two **Qur'anic proofs**; why **Ḥalq is superior to Taqṣīr**; how a **bald person** fulfils it; the ruling for a **muḥṣar** (blocked pilgrim); **women's Taqṣīr**; and the **amount, time, and place** of Ḥalq.

---

## Part 1 — Is Ḥalq Wājib? (Ḥanafī vs. Shāfiʿī)

- **Imam al-Shāfiʿī's position:** once a pilgrim performs the **Ramī of Jamrat al-ʿAqabah** on the 10th, he **exits the restrictions of iḥrām except two** — **intimacy with women** and **perfume** — and the rest are lifted **by the Ramī alone**. On this view, **Ḥalq/Qaṣr is not wājib**.
- **Ḥanafī position:** **Ḥalq/Qaṣr is wājib** — a required act, not merely a formality that drops away with Ramī.

---

## Part 2 — The Ḥanafī Evidences (Ḥalq is Wājib)

### Dalīl 1 — Sūrat al-Ḥajj

> **ثُمَّ لْيَقْضُوا تَفَثَهُمْ وَلْيُوفُوا نُذُورَهُمْ وَلْيَطَّوَّفُوا بِالْبَيْتِ الْعَتِيقِ**
>
> *"Then let them end their unkemptness (tafath), fulfil their vows, and circumambulate the Ancient House."*
>
> **Ref:** al-Ḥajj, 22:29.

- **Tafath (تفث)** literally means **dirt / dust / unkemptness**. Here it refers to **removing the hair and nails** — i.e. **shaving the head**, along with **cutting the nails** and **trimming the mustache**.
- This is an **order from the Qur'an** (**"lī-yaqḍū" — let them end/fulfil**) → establishing **wujūb**.

### Dalīl 2 — Sūrat al-Fatḥ

> **لَقَدْ صَدَقَ اللَّهُ رَسُولَهُ الرُّؤْيَا بِالْحَقِّ ۖ لَتَدْخُلُنَّ الْمَسْجِدَ الْحَرَامَ إِن شَاءَ اللَّهُ آمِنِينَ مُحَلِّقِينَ رُءُوسَكُمْ وَمُقَصِّرِينَ لَا تَخَافُونَ**
>
> *"Certainly Allah has fulfilled His Messenger's vision in truth: you will surely enter the Sacred Mosque, if Allah wills, in safety, **with your heads shaved and (hair) shortened**, without fear."*
>
> **Ref:** al-Fatḥ, 48:27.

Allah **fulfilled the dream** of His Messenger ﷺ — the dream of **entering Masjid al-Ḥarām and performing Ṭawāf**. The phrase **"muḥalliqīna ruʾūsakum wa muqaṣṣirīn"** [shaving your heads and shortening] is a **circumstantial clause (ḥāl)** describing the state of entry. The obligation is drawn from it in one of two ways:

**Interpretation A — it carries the sense of a command (amr):**
- Read as an **implied command** ("enter… while shaving/shortening"), it is a strong dalīl for wujūb. Because there is **a slight uncertainty** in the meaning, the Aḥnāf **do not raise it to farḍ** — they settle it at **wājib**.
- **The "in shāʾa Allāh" question:** how can an obligation be tied to **Allah's will**, which is unknown? — **Answer:** the **"in shāʾa Allāh"** attaches to the **safety (āminīn)**, *not* to the command of entering. So the meaning is: "you will surely enter… **shaving/shortening** — in **safety**, if Allah wills." It concerns the **security**, not the order to shave. (It also functions as a **tabarruk / taʿabbud** phrase — referring the matter to Allah's knowledge — not literal conditionality.)

**Interpretation B — it is information / a promise (khabar):**
- Taken as **khabar** (Allah **informing** that they will enter shaving/shortening), it is still a proof, because **Allah's information always comes true** — it is effectively a **promise**.
- For the promise to be **guaranteed**, note that shaving/shortening is a **human action** (under human control, sometimes done, sometimes not). The only way Allah's information about it is **certain to occur** is if the action is **obligatory** — obligation **compels** it to happen. Therefore the āyah **demands wujūb**. (On this reading, "in shāʾa Allāh" is again **not literal conditionality** but referral to Allah's knowledge — since Allah is informing that it will certainly be.)

**"wa" = "aw" (or):** in **"muḥalliqīna… wa muqaṣṣirīn,"** the **wāw** carries the meaning of **aw (or)** — **Ḥalq and Taqṣīr are both permissible**; some pilgrims shave, some shorten. The two cannot both be done by the same person at once.

## Part 3 — Ḥalq is Superior to Taqṣīr (Afḍal)

The Prophet ﷺ **supplicated for the muḥalliqīn (those who shaved) three times**, and for the **muqaṣṣirīn (those who shortened) once**:

> **«اللَّهُمَّ ارْحَمِ الْمُحَلِّقِينَ» (ثَلَاثًا) … «وَالْمُقَصِّرِينَ»**
>
> *"O Allah, have mercy on those who shaved"* (three times), then *"…and those who shortened."*
>
> **(hadith as cited in class — confirm number in hadith index; narrated in Bukhārī & Muslim)**

**Reasoning:** **Ḥalq is the additional act** — shaving **includes** shortening **plus** removing the hair to the root; when the head is shaved completely, the hair is necessarily "shortened" to the utmost degree.

### Reconciling the hadith "whoever did Ramī became ḥalāl"

A narration reports the Prophet ﷺ saying on the minbar that **whoever performed the Ramī became ḥalāl**, **without mentioning Ḥalq**. This must be reconciled with the two āyāt above:

- **We read Ḥalq into it** — otherwise the statement would **contradict the Kitāb of Allah**, which is not acceptable; the statement must accord with the Qur'an.
- **Or:** it mentions only the **major part (aktharuhā)** — after **Qurbānī and Ramī** (the bulk of the acts), a **minor part (Ḥalq)** remains; it is normal in speech to name the **greater part** of something.

## Part 4 — The Bald Person (No Hair)

How does someone with **no hair** (bald, or hair not present) perform Ḥalq/Qaṣr?

- He simply **runs a razor (or trimmer) over his head** — obtaining the **mushābahah (resemblance)** of shaving. This is **attributed to the Prophet ﷺ** (a ḥadīth).
- **Logic:** he is **not able** to literally shave (no hair), but he **is able** to achieve the **resemblance** by running the razor — so he acts **according to his capacity**, and that suffices, per the principle: **مَا لَا يُدْرَكُ كُلُّهُ لَا يُتْرَكُ كُلُّهُ** [what cannot be attained in full is not left in full].

### Removing hair by chemical / cream

- Using a **depilatory cream or chemical** to remove the hair **is sufficient**, because **Ḥalq is the removal of hair** — it does not matter whether it is done by **razor** or by **cream/chemical**.
- **But the razor is the recommended (afḍal) option** — it is the **practice of the Prophet ﷺ**, and the word **"ḥalq" literally means shaving with a razor**. (The Prophet ﷺ is reported to have used a hair-removing paste — *nūra* — type substance as well; today's creams serve the same purpose, but the **razor is afḍal**.)

## Part 5 — The Muḥṣar (Blocked Pilgrim)

A **muḥṣar (محصر)** is one **prevented / blocked** from completing his Hajj (the full rulings of **Iḥṣār** come in a later chapter). Is **Ḥalq wājib** on him?

| View | Ḥalq on the muḥṣar |
|------|--------------------|
| **Abū Ḥanīfah & Muḥammad** | **Not wājib** over him — he becomes ḥalāl **without** it; if he removes the "dirt" some other way (e.g. a **perfumed soap/plant**), there is **no penalty**, since he committed no violation |
| **Imam Abū Yūsuf** | **Wājib** over him — so if he uses something perfumed on his head, a **penalty** is due, because he is **still in iḥrām** until he performs Ḥalq |
| **Muṣannif's preferred view** | **Ḥalq is compulsory** on him too; he **cannot become ḥalāl without Ḥalq/Qaṣr** — so if he violates before it (e.g. washes his head with a **perfumed plant/soap**), a **penalty** is due |

## Part 6 — Women: Taqṣīr Only

- **A woman's option is Taqṣīr (shortening)** — **Ḥalq is prohibited** for her.
- **Why:** shaving would **disfigure her natural shape (fiṭrah)** — women are **ordered to keep the hair** on their heads; the hair is part of their natural **zīnah**. This is from the **Sunnah** and the practice of the **Azwāj al-Muṭahharāt (the pure wives) رضي الله عنهن**.
- **Amount:** she shortens by about **a fingertip (qadr al-unmulah)** — roughly **one inch** — **from all sides** of the hair.
- A **weak opinion** (attributed to some) that a woman also takes a little — **is not approved** by the evidences of the Sharia.
- **The beard** has nothing to do with this (it concerns head hair only).

### Note on men and the beard

- **Shaving the beard disfigures the natural (fiṭrī) shape of a man** — the **zīnah of a man is in his beard**.
- The **Islamic way**: the beard should be **one fistful (qabḍah)**; the **extra beyond a fist may be trimmed** — as opposed to those who let it grow without limit, or those who shave it off.

## Part 7 — The Amount of Ḥalq and Qaṣr

### Ḥalq (shaving)

- **Best (afḍal):** shave the **whole head**. Used absolutely, **"ḥalaqa"** refers to the **entire head** — and the Prophet ﷺ **shaved his whole head**.
- **Obligatory minimum:** **one-fourth (rubʿ) of the head** — because **one-fourth represents the whole head** (as in the **masḥ of the head in wuḍū'**, where one-fourth stands for the whole).
- If he shaves **less than one-fourth**, it is **not sufficient** — he **remains under the restrictions of iḥrām**.
- **Begin from the right side** — the Prophet ﷺ **distributed his hair from the right** among the people (it is his blessed hair), and gave the **left side's hair** to **Umm Sulaym / Abū Ṭalḥah** specifically (as in the ḥadīth).
- If he shaves **only one-fourth**, it **suffices** and he becomes ḥalāl, but it is **makrūh** — against the Prophet's ﷺ practice of shaving the whole head.

### The order on Yawm al-Naḥr (Ramī → Dhabḥ → Ḥalq)

On the 10th the three acts are done in order:

1. **Ramī** (of Jamrat al-ʿAqabah);
2. **Dhabḥ (ذبح)** [slaughter] — **if due** (on the **Mutamattiʿ / Qārin**; not on the Mufrid);
3. **Ḥalq**.

The word **"thumma (ثُمَّ)"** [then] indicates **tartīb (sequence)** → the **tartīb is wājib** per the Imam. Missing the order **without ʿudhr** incurs a **penalty**.

### Taqṣīr (shortening) — for men who choose it

- **Amount:** about **one fingertip (unmulah)** ≈ **one inch**, **from all sides** of the head.
- **Strong recommendation:** **go a little beyond** one fingertip (out of **iḥtiyāṭ** / to be certain), because the **hair is not equal in length** on all sides — a flat "one fingertip" cut might leave some shorter hairs cut by **less than an inch**. Going beyond gives **certainty** that **all** the hair has been shortened by at least an inch, from every side.
- The **finger-joint mark** (the crease on the finger past the tip) is the practical gauge — it is **approximately one inch**.

## Part 8 — The Time and Place of Ḥalq

| Imam | Restricted by time? | Restricted by place? |
|------|--------------------|----------------------|
| **Imam Abū Ḥanīfah** *(preferred)* | **Yes** — within the **three days of Naḥr** | **Yes** — inside the **Ḥaram** |
| **Imam Abū Yūsuf** | No | No |
| **Imam Muḥammad** | No | **Yes** (place only) |

- Per **Abū Ḥanīfah**, one who performs Ḥalq **after the days** or **outside the Ḥaram** — **intentionally, without ʿudhr** — incurs a **penalty** (like any missed wājib).

### The dalīls

**For "no restriction of place" (opponents):** the **Companions did Ḥalq at Ḥudaybiyah**, and Ḥudaybiyah is (claimed to be) **outside the Ḥaram**.
> **Ḥanafī reply:** **part** of Ḥudaybiyah is in the **Ḥaram** and **part in the Ḥill** — so this is **not a certain proof**. The Prophet's ﷺ **stay** was in the **Ḥill** part, but he **prayed and performed the acts of worship in the Ḥaram** part — indicating the ʿibādah (including Ḥalq) was done in the **Ḥaram**.

**For "no restriction of time" (opponents):** the ḥadīth in which a man on the day of Naḥr asked, *"I did such-and-such before/after (its order),"* and the Prophet ﷺ replied **"لَا حَرَجَ" ("no harm")**.
> **Ḥanafī reply:** (1) this **"lā ḥaraj"** was a **concession** in the **early phase** of the Sharia, while the rulings were being **taught**; and (2) **"no harm" means no sin (in the Hereafter)** — it does **not** mean "**no penalty**." Compare the one who **shaves due to a head ailment** — the Qur'an says **"no harm,"** yet a **fidyah** is still due:
>
> > **فَمَن كَانَ مِنكُم مَّرِيضًا أَوْ بِهِ أَذًى مِّن رَّأْسِهِ فَفِدْيَةٌ مِّن صِيَامٍ أَوْ صَدَقَةٍ أَوْ نُسُكٍ**
> >
> > *"…whoever of you is ill or has an ailment of the head [and shaves], then a ransom of fasting, charity, or sacrifice."*
> >
> > **Ref:** al-Baqarah, 2:196.
>
> So the **Hajj is valid** (the farāʾiḍ are covered) and there is **no sin in the Hereafter**, but the **worldly penalty** for the missed wājib **remains**.

**Abū Ḥanīfah's dalīl (preferred):** the **practice of the Prophet ﷺ** — he did Ḥalq **in the Ḥaram, within the days**. His action is the **commentary (tafsīr)** of the Qur'an, and he ordered: **«خُذُوا عَنِّي مَنَاسِكَكُمْ»** *"Take from me your rites."* Therefore the **restriction of place and time is wājib**, and violating it (without ʿudhr) brings the **penalty of a missed wājib**.

**Case:** a person **left the Ḥaram**, then remembered he had **not done Ḥalq in the Ḥaram**, **returned** and did it there → **no penalty**, because he **achieved the place restriction**.

## Q&A from Class

**Q:** Are both **Ḥalq and Qaṣr** available for **men**? — **A:** **Yes**, both are available, but **Ḥalq is afḍal**.

**Q:** When men choose **Taqṣīr**, how much? — **A:** about **one fingertip (≈ one inch)**, **all over the head, from every side**. Better to **go a bit beyond** the fingertip so he is **satisfied that every side is cut by at least an inch**, since hair length is uneven around the head. (The **finger-joint** past the fingertip is the practical measure — roughly one inch.)
