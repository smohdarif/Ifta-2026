## Part 1 — The Meaning of Ramī al-Jimār

- **Linguistically**, **Ramī al-Jimār** means **throwing the small pebbles (jimār)**. Two things are built into the term:
  - the objects must be **small pebbles**, and
  - there must be **actual throwing (ramī)**.
- **Throwing is essential (farḍ of the act):** merely **placing** the pebble on the Jamrah is **not valid**. There must be a genuine throw.
- **A very light throw** — like just **pushing** the pebble away from oneself — **is sufficient** (it still counts as throwing), **but the masnūn (Sunnah) way is a proper throw**: throwing with the force needed to carry the pebble across the distance to the Jamrah.

---

## Part 2 — Niyābah (Performing Ramī by Proxy)

- A person should perform the Ramī **himself**.
- **If he is unable** — due to **old age, illness**, or similar — **someone else may throw on his behalf**, because in Hajj **niyābah (نيابة)** [acting as a proxy] is **permissible**, especially for Ramī.
- The incapable person may **place the pebbles in his own palm**, and another person throws them for him — this is valid. Once he is unable, it does not matter whether he throws or another throws on his behalf.
- **Parallels already studied:**
  - In **Ṭawāf**, a person who cannot walk may be **carried**.
  - In the **Wuqūf at Muzdalifah**, a person may be **carried** to fulfil it.

## Part 3 — The Time of Ramī: Four Days

Ramī is performed over **four days**: the **10th** (Yawm al-Naḥr) and then the **11th, 12th, and 13th** of Dhū al-Ḥijjah. The **10th is different** from the other days in its timing.

### 3.1 — The 10th (Yawm al-Naḥr): only Jamrat al-ʿAqabah

On the 10th, **only Jamrat al-ʿAqabah** is stoned. The day divides into **four periods**:

| Period | Ruling |
|--------|--------|
| **From Ṭulūʿ al-Fajr → sunrise (Ṭulūʿ al-Shams)** | **Jāʾiz but makrūh** |
| **From sunrise → Zawāl** | **Mustaḥabb** (the recommended time) |
| **From Zawāl → sunset (Ghurūb)** | **Jāʾiz, without karāhah** |
| **The night (between 10th and 11th)** | **Jāʾiz but makrūh** if without ʿudhr; **no karāhah** if there is an ʿudhr |

- **Start:** the time **begins at Ṭulūʿ al-Fajr** (Ḥanafī view) — so it **cannot** be done in the **night before** the 10th (the night of the 9th/10th).
- **The night after** counts as part of the 10th: a Ramī "day" runs from **one Fajr to the next Fajr** (a full 24-hour reckoning), so the night between the 10th and 11th still belongs to the 10th.
- **End:** the time for the 10th's Ramī **ends at sunset (Ghurūb)** of the 10th; the night is still valid but **makrūh**.
- **Delaying to the next day (11th) without ʿudhr → penalty (dam)**.

#### The differing views on the start time

| Scholar | Start of the 10th's Ramī |
|---------|--------------------------|
| **Ḥanafī** *(preferred)* | **Ṭulūʿ al-Fajr** (basic time); **sunrise** onward is mustaḥabb |
| **Imam al-Shāfiʿī** | From the **second half of the night** before the 10th |
| **Sufyān al-Thawrī** | **Not** permissible before **sunrise** |

> **Note:** the Ḥanafī–Shāfiʿī disagreement over "does the time begin in the night before, or after Fajr of the day?" recurs in **three** places: the **timing of Ramī**, the **Wuqūf at ʿArafah**, and the **Wuqūf at Muzdalifah**. Pilgrims should know the Hajj timings **according to their own school** and not blindly follow another's practice without realising it.

#### Ḥanafī dalīl

The Prophet ﷺ sent the **weaker members and women** of his family ahead from Muzdalifah in the night, and instructed them **not to stone Jamrat al-ʿAqabah until they reached the morning**:

> **«لَا تَرْمُوا الْجَمْرَةَ حَتَّى تُصْبِحُوا»**
>
> *"Do not stone the Jamrah until you reach the morning."*
>
> **(hadith as cited in class — confirm wording/number in hadith index; narrated concerning the Banū al-Muṭṭalib / the weak sent ahead from Muzdalifah)**

This proves the time **starts once morning comes** and does **not** begin before **Ṣubḥ** appears — a **counter to both Imam al-Shāfiʿī** (night before) **and Sufyān al-Thawrī** (only after sunrise).

#### Reconciling two apparently conflicting ḥadīths

- One narration: **do not stone until you reach the morning (Ṣubḥ)**.
- Another narration: **do not stone until sunrise (Ṭulūʿ al-Shams)**.

There is an **apparent conflict (taʿāruḍ ẓāhirī)**. The Aḥnāf, as is their method, make **tatbīq (reconciliation)** so both narrations can be acted upon:

- The **first** ḥadīth refers to the **actual/basic time** — it begins at **Fajr**.
- The **second** ḥadīth refers to the **mustaḥabb time** — best performed **after sunrise**.

Since the two ḥadīths speak of **different subjects** (basic time vs. preferred time), there is **no real conflict**.

### 3.2 — The 11th and 12th (Ayyām al-Tashrīq)

The **11th** is the **first day of Ayyām al-Tashrīq (أيام التشريق)**; the **12th** is the **second day**. Their Ramī differs from the 10th in **two** ways:

1. **Time begins after Zawāl** — it is **not jāʾiz** to stone **before Zawāl** on these two days (unlike the 10th, whose time began at Fajr).
2. **All three Jamarāt** are stoned — ** Jamrat al-Ūlā, al-Wusṭā, and al-ʿAqabah** — not just al-ʿAqabah.

- **After sunset until Fajr:** still **jāʾiz but with karāhah** (no penalty).
- A **non-preferred** opinion permits Ramī **before Zawāl** on the 11th/12th; the **preferred** view is that one **must wait until Zawāl**.

**Dalīl (preferred view):** the Prophet's ﷺ **action** — he stoned **al-ʿAqabah on the 10th after sunrise**, and stoned on the **other days after Zawāl**. **Timings are fixed by the texts (naṣṣ), not by qiyās.**

## Part 4 — The First Departure (Nafr al-Awwal) and Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:203

After the Ramī of the **12th** (which is after Zawāl), a pilgrim has a **choice**:

- **Depart Minā for Makkah on the 12th** — this is the **Nafr al-Awwal (النفر الأول)** [first / earlier departure]; **or**
- **Stay for the 13th** (the 4th day), stone that day, then depart — the **Nafr al-Thānī** [second departure].

**It is better (afḍal)** to wait for the **4th day (13th)**, stone, and then depart. The Qur'anic basis:

> **فَمَن تَعَجَّلَ فِي يَوْمَيْنِ فَلَا إِثْمَ عَلَيْهِ وَمَن تَأَخَّرَ فَلَا إِثْمَ عَلَيْهِ ۚ لِمَنِ اتَّقَىٰ**
>
> *"Whoever hastens (to leave) in two days — there is no sin upon him; and whoever delays — there is no sin upon him — for the one who has taqwā."*
>
> **Ref:** al-Baqarah, 2:203.

### Two questions on the āyah

**Q1 — Why "no sin (lā ithm)"?** This wording is normally used for **optional/permissible** matters — yet the 4th day's Ramī (for one who stays) is **wājib**. How can the āyah say "no sin"?

> **Answer:** here **"no sin" does not mean "it is optional / permission granted."** It means the person is **forgiven** — no sin remains upon him — in **both** cases (whether he departs after the 3rd day's Ramī or after the 4th). One who leaves early is **forgiven** the shortfall; one who stays and completes is likewise fine. It is about **maghfirah (forgiveness)**, not about declaring the act optional.

**Q2 — Why is "for the one who has taqwā (لِمَنِ اتَّقَىٰ)" attached only to the one who *delays* (wa man taʾakhkhara)?** It should apply to the one who **hastens** too — indeed, the hastening one arguably needs the reminder more.

> **Answer:** the clause **belongs to both**. Both the one who hastens and the one who delays are **encouraged toward taqwā**, and it is **their choice** to stay for three days or four.

**Other interpretations of the taqwā clause:** some say the **taqwā** here refers to **keeping away from hunting (ṣayd) in the Ḥaram** (also mentioned in the surrounding passage), not to the Ramī; others take it as **taqwā throughout the whole of Hajj (and the rest of life)**, or **keeping away from the restrictions of iḥrām**.

### Losing the choice: when the 13th's Ramī becomes obligatory

If a pilgrim completed the Ramī of the 10th, 11th, and 12th and **could have departed** on the 12th but **stayed**, and the **Fajr of the 13th arose while he was still in Minā**, then his **option to skip the 13th is gone** — the **Ramī of the 13th now becomes wājib** on him.

- So to leave under the **Nafr al-Awwal**, he must **depart on the 12th before the Fajr of the 13th enters**; otherwise the **13th's Ramī is obligatory**.

## Part 5 — The Time of Ramī on the 13th (the 4th Day)

This is the **third day of Tashrīq** (counting from the 11th).

| View | Ruling on the 13th | Basis |
|------|--------------------|-------|
| **Imam Abū Ḥanīfah** *(preferred)* | **Recommended after Zawāl**, but **permitted before Zawāl** (after Fajr) — **specific to this last day** | See dalīl below |
| **The Ṣāḥibayn (Abū Yūsuf & Muḥammad)** | Must be **after Zawāl**, like the 11th/12th | The Prophet ﷺ stoned "on the (remaining) days after Zawāl" |

**Imam Abū Ḥanīfah's dalīl:**

1. **Naqlī (primary):** the statement of **Ibn ʿAbbās رضي الله عنهما** that the time of the **last day's** Ramī **begins when the day (Fajr) begins**. In a matter that **cannot be derived by qiyās**, a **Ṣaḥābī's statement has the ruling of a marfūʿ ḥadīth** (attributed to the Prophet ﷺ). The general ḥadīth "the Prophet ﷺ stoned on the remaining days after Zawāl" is understood to **exclude the 4th day** on the strength of Ibn ʿAbbās's report (or that ḥadīth describes only what is **recommended** on that day).
2. **ʿAqlī (supportive):** the 4th day's Ramī is a duty that **may be skipped entirely** (one may depart on the 12th). Since a **concession is given to skip it completely**, a concession should also be given to **perform it early** (before Zawāl).

> **Method note:** how to recognise the **preferred opinion** in a book like *al-Hidāyah* / *Badāʾiʿ* — the author places the **dalīl of the preferred view last**. Here the dalīl of **Imam Abū Ḥanīfah comes last**, marking it as the preferred position.

## Part 6 — Distance and Hitting the Jamrah

- **There is no fixed limit** on how far one stands from the Jamrah.
- **What counts:** the pebble's **trajectory must go toward the Jamrah** and **fall within its designated boundary/basin**. **Hitting the pillar exactly is not compulsory** — landing **within the fixed area** is sufficient.
- If the throw goes **elsewhere** (not toward the Jamrah, or outside its area), it does **not** count.
- **Recommended distance:** about **five arm-lengths (hands)** from the Jamrah — from the Prophet's ﷺ practice — but since not everyone can manage this in the crowd, throwing **from further** is fine, **as long as the pebble lands in the fixed area**.

## Q&A from Class

**Q:** Imam Abū Ḥanīfah's *ʿaqlī* argument — "since the 4th day's Ramī may be skipped entirely (rukhṣah), it may also be done early" — could this same reasoning be applied to **combining the prayers**? For example, a traveller may pray the full or **qaṣr**; so if he prays at the earlier time, why can he not also combine **ʿAṣr** into **Ẓuhr's** time?

**A:** No — because that **ʿaqlī (qiyās) argument here is only a *supportive* argument**; the **primary** proof is the **naqlī** one (Ibn ʿAbbās's report). A **supportive** argument **cannot be applied** where it **conflicts with a naqlī proof** — and the **times of ṣalāh are fixed by naṣṣ**, so a prayer cannot be brought forward before its time. Only in a mas'alah where there is **no naqlī evidence at all** does **qiyās become the main argument**. So questions built on the *ʿaqlī* argument should be understood in light of its being **merely supportive**, not universally extendable.
